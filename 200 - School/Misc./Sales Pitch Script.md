Sharks, let me ask you something. When you learned to drive, did reading a manual make you feel ready to get behind the wheel? Of course not. Real skills require real experience. But what if you could get that experience safely, repeatedly, and from anywhere in the world? That is exactly what Immersify delivers.

Today, over 220 million university students worldwide are learning critical skills medical procedures, software engineering workflows, public speaking, laboratory techniques almost entirely through passive methods: lectures, textbooks, and two-dimensional videos.

Research from the University of Maryland shows that students retain up to 75% more information through immersive, hands-on learning compared to passive instruction. Yet the infrastructure to deliver that does not exist at scale. Until now.

Immersify is a cloud-based SaaS platform that delivers structured, curriculum-aligned skill modules inside virtual reality environments. We partner directly with universities to embed our platform into their existing learning management systems Moodle, Canvas, Blackboard.

A student studying IT system administration puts on a VR headset and walks through configuring a live server rack. A medical student rehearses a surgical procedure repeatedly without risk. A law student argues a moot court case in front of a virtual panel.

Our platform works across Meta Quest, HTC Vive, and browser-based WebXR meaning students with no headset can still participate via desktop in a reduced-immersion mode. No one is excluded.

Our freemium model gives students free access to a rotating library of 20 foundational skill modules. Premium tier is priced at €12.99 per student per month, unlocking 300+ advanced modules, AI-driven performance analytics, and instructor dashboards.

We also license at the institutional level: universities pay a flat annual API integration fee of €8,000 per faculty, giving all enrolled students premium access included in their tuition bundle.

We are currently in beta with two universities in Finland and Estonia, with 1,400 active users and €18,000 in monthly recurring revenue after just four months.
